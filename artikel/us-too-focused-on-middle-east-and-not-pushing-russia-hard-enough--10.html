Volodymyr Zelensky has voiced deep frustration with the US, saying delays in delivering vital anti-ballistic missiles are costing Ukrainian lives and complaining that the US is not pushing Russia hard enough in peace talks.

In an interview with the BBC at his central Kyiv office, Ukraine's president also said it was "strange" that Donald Trump had posted a photo of himself embracing Vladimir Putin as "my friend".

It has been revealed that "Of course it's up to the President of the United States, but... it's strange," Zelensky remarked, speaking in English.

Since a bruising encounter with Trump in the Oval Office in 2025, Zelensky has been careful how he handles relations with the United States.

In a recent development, trump's two envoys have been trying to negotiate a peace deal between Russia and Ukraine - to end the huge loss of life. But they often seem more in tune with Moscow's position, than that of Kyiv.

The latest round of peace talks, mediated in Abu Dhabi earlier this month, made no obvious progress: "The more we know, the more problems", is how Zelensky described it.

He noted talks were expected to resume in December.

On Thursday, Donald Trump posted on his Truth Social platform that at his own summit with Putin in China last week he had urged Russia to "pull back all military aggression against territories throughout Europe" and said he believed a peace deal with Ukraine was "a strong possibility".

Ukraine urgently needs the talks to move forward and bring five exhausting years of Russia's full-scale invasion to an end.

Russian strikes across the country have been growing more frequent and more deadly: the number of civilians being killed is at an all-time high.

So Zelensky called for "real helpful allies, who can push Russia" to achieve results and urged the US to live up to its reputation as a strong country with "power and respect".

Industry observers note that "When you try to be very neutral with the aggressor... it means you support victims and you support also aggressor. It's not right," he said.

The US argues that it is neutral: in the complex search for a peace deal between the two sides that can hold.

In the meantime, Kyiv is struggling to defend its skies.

The air alerts, day and night, are now a draining feature of everyday life. As Russian ballistic missiles target power stations and bridges, jet-powered drones are rolled out daily at central Kyiv and other big cities.

Ukraine rolls out hundreds of drones at targets in Russia in response.

Zelensky noted the military here are now able to destroy about 70% of Russia's fastest drones, a statistic he said was improving.

But there is a "very, very big" deficit of interceptors to stop ballistic missiles and he stated the Americans were delaying deliveries of its Patriot systems.

According to recent reports, "Technically SpaceX, politically - I don't know, I really don't know," Zelensky replied. "It's like... ping pong. We are asking one side, they say it's the other.".

Ukraine's own long-range strikes against Russian targets have increased in recent weeks, including oil refineries and ships carrying weapons or fuel.

On Thursday, Italy's Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said an attack that sank a Russian warship docked in eastern Libya this week risked spreading war to the Mediterranean, which was unacceptable.

Zelensky defended the move, calling the warship Merkury an "absolutely fair goal".

He accepted that such strikes were "very dangerous" but stated Ukraine's forces "never attack" without co-ordinating with its partners.

He declined to be more specific.

But he acknowledged that Ukraine was bracing for a difficult and dangerous winter.

Recent attacks on Kyiv have targeted bridges over the vast river Dnipro, apparently trying to cut the city in two.

Some residents have already left town for the winter and businesses closed, as power cuts and strikes on logistics networks have pushed operating costs unmanageably high.

They have also plunged much of the city into darkness.

"[Russia] wants us to run away, of course," Zelensky said.

"People can go, but we will try to defend our capital in any way. We need our capital. Without a capital, you don't have a country.".
